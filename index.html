03 — Selected Work

Three projects, told as cases — not bullet points.

Case study · Sony Pictures Imageworks

SPNote — from feasibility study to 300+ users, and an Innovation Award

It started as a question, not a mandate: could a single internal media notebook replace the scattered, unofficial workflows artists were using? I ran the feasibility study myself, then taught myself Electron — novice to first working deliverable in two weeks.

The part most engineers skip is the part that made it work. I pushed to include real user research and had to fight to get those meetings on the calendar — sitting down with users across five departments and three department heads to learn what they actually needed, not what a spec assumed. That research reshaped the roadmap, which I then had to deliver under real time pressure.

The tool reached 300+ internal users in under eight months and won the Sony Imageworks Innovation Award. But the piece I'm proudest of is the legacy: before I left, I ran a crash course in Electron for the department so the work would outlive me — teams launched new projects on it after I was gone.

Case study · Independent business

SparkForge Workshop — a business built on a constraint I wouldn't relax

SparkForge is a sustainable-goods business I started and run end to end — product, brand, storefront, supply chain, and growth. The defining decision was a sourcing rule I set on day one and never loosened: no Amazon; Canadian and secondhand suppliers only. That constraint made everything harder and the business more honest.

Within that box, the numbers moved. Gross revenue grew 928.6% year over year to $7,500. I drove COGS down from 34% to 25% by reworking suppliers and unit economics, and expanded from 2 to 11 markets. I built the e-commerce site myself and grew its traffic 27% YoY.

Case study · Published research

Effective Community Action Model — a peer-reviewed climate tool

I designed, researched, and programmed a user-friendly climate model in JavaScript and Electron that lets people reason about the impact of community-level climate action. I then wrote it up as a scientific paper, published in The Expedition UBC, a peer-reviewed journal — closing the loop from build to defensible, published research.

It's a small, concrete example of the whole thesis: technical work is only finished when the people who need it can understand and act on it.
